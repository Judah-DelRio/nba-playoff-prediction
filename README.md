# NBA Playoff Prediction

Predicting playoff success from regular-season team statistics across 29 NBA seasons (1997-2025, 862 team-seasons).

**Headline results:** an XGBoost + OLS pipeline reaching a cross-validated R² of 0.719, and R² of 0.559 on a fully held-out 2024-25 season, where it placed Oklahoma City, the eventual champion, in the Finals.

## Key findings

1. **Winning is most of the signal.** Regular-season win percentage alone reaches a cross-validated R² of 0.581. The engineered features (Four Factors, the other team statistics, playoff seeding, and late-season form) add 13.8 points of R², bringing the model to 0.719.
2. **Regular-season injury data added almost no predictive value.** A full injury dataset (1997-2025) was engineered into features and raised cross-validated R² by only 0.001.
3. **Single seasons vary widely.** Holding out each of the 29 seasons in turn, R² ranged from 0.425 (2023) to 0.887 (2017), with a mean of 0.719. The 2025 holdout (0.559) is the second-lowest season, and every season from 2020 through 2025 falls below the mean, so the model is less accurate on recent seasons than its overall average.
4. **Efficiency beats style** (from the v1 regression study): Net Rating dominates every box-score stat, a one-standard-deviation increase predicting ~0.8 additional playoff rounds. True Shooting absorbs three-point percentage once both are in the model, and pace carries a negative coefficient. Steph Curry didn't break basketball; smart scoring has always reigned.

## Project evolution

| Version | Scope | Method | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1 (Excel) | 11 seasons, 330 team-seasons | Nested multiple regression, standardized predictors, Playoff Predictor Index | R² = 0.52; >50% exact-round accuracy |
| v2 (Python) | 29 seasons, 862 team-seasons | Feature-engineered pipeline, OLS + XGBoost, season-level cross-validation + 2025 holdout | CV R² = 0.719, holdout R² = 0.559 |

The v1 study tested three hypotheses in sequence: that 3P% is the best traditional predictor (confirmed), that TS% supersedes it (confirmed), and that Net Rating dominates both (confirmed, final R² 0.52). The v2 rebuild expanded the window to 29 seasons, added engineered features (Dean Oliver's Four Factors, playoff seeding, late-season form, injury integration), and replaced single-equation regression with a validated ML pipeline.

## Method (v2)

- **Target:** playoff round reached, 0 (missed playoffs) through 5 (champion)
- **Seasons:** assigned from the NBA game ID, so each season runs October through June (2025 means 2024-25)
- **Regular season:** only games that count in the standings, so NBA Cup finals, play-in games, and playoff games are excluded from the features
- **Features:** Four Factors, seeding, late-season form, injury aggregates
- **Models:** OLS baseline and XGBoost, compared under 5-fold cross-validation that holds out whole seasons, with features scaled on training data only
- **Validation:** leave-one-season-out across all 29 seasons, and the 2024-25 season held out entirely from training

## Data

Full datasets are not committed. The v2 notebook uses the [NBA Dataset: Box Scores and Stats (1947 - Today)](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/eoinamoore/historical-nba-data-and-player-box-scores) on Kaggle (Eoin A. Moore) for team, game, and player statistics. The v1 Excel study used [Basketball Reference](https://www.basketball-reference.com/). The injury data builds on [NBA Player Injury Stats (1951-2023)](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/loganlauton/nba-injury-stats-1951-2023) on Kaggle (Lauton, 2023), but it extends past 2023, so its full source is still being confirmed (TBD). `data/README.md` documents the files the notebook expects; place them in `data/` to reproduce the pipeline.
